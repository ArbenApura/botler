# Botler architecture

Summary of the decisions on plan BTL-20261021-0458 (https://afb.arbenger.com/arbenger/BTL/BTL-20261021-0458). The board is the plan of record; this file is the short version for people opening the repo.

## Shape

```
Flutter app (Android) <--HTTPS--> SvelteKit server (botler.arbenger.com) --> PostgreSQL + pgvector
                                        |-- ops layer (one set of verbs)
                                        |-- agent loop --> DeepSeek or Anthropic (through the proxy)
                                        |-- the notebook, chat summaries and jobs
                                        '-- FCM push
```

- **App** (`app/`): Flutter, Android first. Chat-first single screen with a summary card. Reminders and alarms are scheduled on the phone so they work offline; a sync contract replaces them when the server changes. Writes go through an on-device outbox with a client `requestId`.
- **Server** (`server/`): SvelteKit 3, Svelte 5 runes, TypeScript strict, Drizzle, Better Auth with Google sign-in (closed to an allow-list).
- **Database**: its own PostgreSQL 17 with pgvector, in its own container and volume on the VPS. Every table has `user_id`; forced row level security is generated from a registry; requests run as the non-owning `app_runtime` role inside a transaction-local tenant context.
- **One operations layer**: the HTTP API, the agent tools and the background jobs call the same verbs. Writes are idempotent by `requestId`. Verbs are `free` (record, and add, change or remove anything in the money plan; owner decision 2026-10-28) or `gated` (a balance count or reset); a gated verb needs an `approvalId` minted only by a user-authenticated HTTP call. After untrusted text, and after the third delete of a message, a free write becomes a card too.

## Money

Integer minor units of each amount's own currency (PHP centavos by default), Asia/Manila dates. Ledger types: income, expense, transfer (with fee), refund, adjustment. Balance = latest snapshot + signed transactions since it. The payday split is proposed by the agent and approved by the user; a shortfall is shown and the user chooses what to cut. Allocation rules: fixed peso, percentage or the rest, in forms or plain words (parsed, shown, approved). A split always sums exactly to the pay.

### Currency, schedules, calendar and audit

- **Settings**: one `user_setting` row per person (currency, language, reply language, week start). Money is formatted and parsed only by `finance/currency.ts` (server) and `core/money.dart` (app); the currency in settings is the MAIN currency, and changing it never converts the money plan.
- **Wallets and rates (BTL-20261023-1407)**: an account holds one wallet per currency (`account_wallet`); a balance is the latest count of that wallet plus the entries in its currency since. A move between two currencies stores what was sent and what arrived (`to_amount`), so the person's own rate is kept. `fx_rate` holds the exchange rates (same for everyone, never deleted from, fetched from the fixed https `FX_RATES_URL` with a global claim, a 3x hold-back per rate and a nightly `fx.refresh` job); reading never waits for the network. `assets_summary` converts every wallet to the main currency and names the wallets with no rate. A bill has its own currency; paying it asks what was really charged when the wallet is in another currency. Amount columns are 64 bit (rupiah, dong, rial).
- **Old app against the new server**: `accounts_balances` keeps `balance`, `countedAt` and `belowMinimum` as the account's own currency wallet (the new `wallets` list and `currency` sit beside them), a request with no `currency` means the account's own, and the new app sends `currency` only when it differs from the usual one, so an ordinary entry is the same request as before. The server is deployed (and migrated) before the app is published. An old app shows a currency it does not know with the peso sign until it is updated.
- **Schedules**: `finance/schedule.ts` models monthly (with last day and every N months), semi-monthly, every N days, weekly, nth weekday, week of month, day range and yearly. Every occurrence is a window `{start, end}`; a single day has start equal to end and the end is the due date. A payment stores its window and matches by overlap, so editing a schedule never makes a paid window look unpaid. Paydays accept single-day kinds only.
- **One due date changed on its own** (BTL-20261022-0650): `bill_override` holds a change to a single due date of a bill, keyed by the date its schedule gives (`original_due_date`): moved up to 31 days, another amount, another paying account, or skipped. The bill, its payments and its other dates stay as they are. `finance/occurrence.ts` (`effectiveOccurrences`, pure) is the one place that applies overrides, and every reader of a bill's dates goes through it via `dueBills` (the Bills screen, the payday split, reminders, the calendar, sync) and `markBillPaid`, which stores the effective window. The free verbs `bill_occurrence_update` (merge: a left-out field keeps its value, null clears it) and `bill_occurrence_reset` are available to the assistant; an amount on a bill whose amount varies turns into an approval card, and a paid date is corrected in the ledger, not here. `bill_update` (all dates) drops the overrides from today on that a new schedule, weekend rule, amount or account replaces, and says how many (`clearedOverrides`). Migration 0018 also carries the audit trigger and the newer `data_reset` function; 0016 and 0017 stay as they ran, and their tests pin them.
- **Calendar** (Botler's own; the Google Calendar sync was removed in BTL-20261022-1311, migration 0019 drops its table and columns): the read verb `calendar_range` (at most 92 days) returns bills as windows, paydays, events, routines, tasks and reminders, plus a labelled estimate of each account's balance over the days. The app draws month, week and agenda views from it and keeps the last range for offline.
- **Time travel (BTL-20261029-0117)**: the read verb `balance_simulate` answers the balance timeline from up to 5 years back to 5 years ahead (at most 1827 days per answer; by day 62 days, by week 2 years), grouped by day, week, month or year. Every number carries its source. **recorded** is the ledger (snapshots and entries loaded once and sliced per instant through `finance/ledger.ts` `balanceOf`; hidden accounts count while they held money). **replay** is today's plan run over each past period from the recorded balance at that period's start, so a period gives the same replay and gap whatever range it was asked in. It counts every payday and bill date, paid or not, at the plan's amount. A payday, bill, goal, rule or account counts only from the day it was made, and the period of the first record replays from the day after it. **simulated** is the future, always run from today's real balances of active accounts through to the end of the range; its flows are exactly `cashflow_forecast`'s. **missed** marks a past payday with no income on its account before the next payday, or an unpaid bill date. A period that holds today is mixed. The pure parts are `finance/billWindows.ts` (bill dates from loaded rows, also used by `dueBills`), `finance/planFlows.ts` (paydays, bills and payday split moves in future or replay mode, shared with the forecast), `finance/scenario.ts` (the what-if) and `finance/simulate.ts`. `ops/planData.ts` loads the plan once inside the caller's transaction. A what-if (switch ids off, other amounts, skipped payday dates, bills late, deferrable bills deferred, at most 50 pretend entries in the main currency) is never saved and changes only the replay and the simulation; ids that are not the caller's are named back as ignored. Every period also says what was spent and received in it (BTL-20261029-0640): spent is expenses plus transfer fees minus refunds (`finance/ledger.ts`: a transfer between own accounts is never spending), received is income, a negative balance count is untracked and apart, each row is converted on its own at the stored rate and a currency with no rate is named in the period's `leftOut`; the replay and the simulation count the plan's money out and in, never its moves. `view: detail` (at most 366 days) adds the entries and the spending of the range per bill (else category, else other) per source as `breakdown`; with `limit` (1 to 100, plus `offset` and an optional `groupKey`) it returns one page of the entries in one fixed order and `entriesTotal`, and without `limit` it keeps the older answer of up to 500 entries so an older app still works. The paging fields are not offered to the assistant (`agentOmit` in `ops/registry.ts`). The assistant's answer is cut in steps (entries, wallets, then period details) to stay under 13,000 characters. The app's Time travel screen (`app/lib/features/simulate/`, route `/simulate`) always says which part of time is shown: a band with words and an icon, solid recorded and dashed simulated or replayed lines, and outlined, tagged simulated entries. It keeps the last plain answer per zoom for offline use, and a data reset clears it.
- **Audit**: triggers on 16 tenant tables write `audit_event` rows in the same transaction, with actor, channel, approval and turn read from transaction-local settings. `account_history` replays a running balance; `audit_verify` walks the chain; `audit_export` is person-only. `data_reset` erases by scope through a SECURITY DEFINER function and leaves one tombstone row.
- **Live chat** (BTL-20261022-0738): `POST /api/v1/agent/chat` answers as server-sent events. Kept with a finished message and replayed: `tool`, `card`, `fallback` (the `STORED` allow-list in `agent/chat.ts`). Live only, never stored, replayed or logged: `start` (sent as soon as the message is claimed, before memory or the model), `phase` (`thinking` at the start of each model call, `writing` at its first word), `reasoning` (the model's thinking), `tool_start` (`{id, name}` only, never the arguments; every one gets a `tool` event with the same `id`). Then `delta`, `done` and `error` as before. A `: ping` comment goes out every 10 s so the phone can tell a quiet stream from a dead one (the app gives up after 45 s of nothing). A claim error (a reused id, bad input) is still an ordinary HTTP error: the claim and the first event happen before the Response exists. The response says `content-encoding: identity` and `no-transform`, and the Caddy site block compresses only listed types, because a compressing proxy holds small pieces back. Thinking is on for the streamed chat bucket (never memory jobs) unless `DEEPSEEK_THINKING` or `ANTHROPIC_THINKING` is 0/false: DeepSeek gets `thinking` and `reasoning_effort` from `CHAT_EFFORT` (low and medium map to high), Anthropic gets adaptive thinking (`display: summarized`) with `output_config.effort` and its tools still offered; `max_tokens` is `CHAT_MAX_TOKENS` (default 64000, thinking shares it with the answer); no temperature is sent; it counts against the monthly cap and the per-message `TURN_TOKEN_BUDGET`. Anthropic's signed thinking blocks go back unchanged in the same message's tool rounds (`ChatMessage.wire_blocks`, never masked again) and are never stored or put in history. `reasoning_content` goes back to DeepSeek in later tool rounds like content: kept with real values restored (`Completion.reasoningRaw`) and masked again by `maskMessages` with the turn's masker, so the placeholders are the same ones DeepSeek saw (masking the wire text itself would rewrite `[NAME_1]` into `[NAME-1]`). A round cut off by the limit (`finish_reason: length`) never runs its tool calls; it ends with `stopped: 'length'` and is stored as failed. The tool rounds of a finished message are stored with it (`chat_turn.transcript`) and never sent to the app.
- **Chat history** (BTL-20261022-0827): the chat is empty on every app start unless the app asks, because the screen state lives only in memory while every turn is already stored in `chat_turn`. `chat_history` (registry kind `person`, so the assistant is never offered it; `limit` 1 to 30, default 20) is a plain read inside the tenant context: no model call, no tokens, and the model's own prompt is unchanged (the model's own history is separate: see Long chats under Agent; `tests/agent/history.test.ts` pins its token bound). It returns the newest turns oldest first, never a running turn, with `clientMessageId` and `status`; stored events are validated one by one (an odd one is skipped, never the call), at most 12 per turn, tool `data` over 4000 characters dropped, the whole answer kept under about 200 KB (old tool data goes first, then the oldest turns). A card event carries the action's status now (`pending`, `applied`, `rejected`, `failed`, `expired`, or `gone` for a missing action); a pending card past its time is reported `expired` although nothing wrote that yet. The app calls it once per run from the chat screen (`ChatNotifier.restore()`, flag in the notifier, reset when another person signs in), keeps the result apart from the live items, shows it read-only (no Undo, no figures that are not in the stored data, only a live pending card answerable) and drops a turn it already sent in this run. A photo message comes back as its typed text only; thinking is never stored, so none comes back.

### Egress wall

The fetch tool refuses private addresses in code, and `deploy/vps/egress.sh` is the second wall: a firewall inside botler-app's own network namespace that rejects new connections to private, link-local (metadata) and carrier-grade ranges and to the Docker host, with one exception, tcp 5432 on botler-db. It works inside the container because AFB's app shares a Docker network with botler-app and the host firewall cannot see same-network traffic. `deploy/vps/egress-test.sh` proves it from inside the container. It runs after every deploy (`deploy.ps1`) and on reboot (cron `@reboot`).

### App updates (BTL-20261022-1428)

The phone is sideloaded, so the VPS delivers new builds. `deploy/vps/release-app.ps1` builds one release APK per processor type (`--split-per-abi`, same versionCode) plus a universal one, checks each (package and versionCode with aapt2, signing certificate against the pinned SHA-256) and with `-Publish` uploads it to the host folder `/srv/botler-releases` (outside `~/botler`, so `deploy.ps1`'s `rsync --delete` never touches it): the APK as `*.part`, size and sha256 verified, renamed, then `latest.json` last, newest three APKs kept. `botler-app` mounts that folder read-only at `/releases` (`BOTLER_RELEASES_DIR`). Two endpoints, both outside the ops layer and the tenant registry (no person's data) and both answering 401 without a session: `GET /api/v1/app/update?versionCode=N&abis=arm64-v8a,armeabi-v7a` (the phone's processor types, best first; the answer carries that split's size and hash and a `downloadPath` ending `?abi=`, or the universal build when none matches or none is sent) returns `{status, latest, downloadPath}` (`none` when nothing newer, no manifest, a bad manifest or a missing or wrong-size APK; never a 500 for a missing folder) and `GET /api/v1/app/update/apk/[versionCode]` streams the file with Range, If-Range, 416 and `no-store`; the file is found by listing the folder against `^botler-\d+\.\d+\.\d+\+\d+(-<abi>)?\.apk$` and never by putting the parameter in a path. Rules live in `server/src/lib/app-update/rules.ts` (pure), handlers in `server/src/lib/server/appUpdate/`. The app (`app/lib/core/app_update.dart`, `state/update.dart`, `features/update/`) re-decides the offer itself and ignores the server's status word: "required" needs a good answer with the phone below `minSupportedCode` and the minimum within the offered build. It downloads only on a tap, hashes while streaming, compares size and sha256, then asks Android (Kotlin `MainActivity`, channel `com.arbenger.botler/update`) to check package, version and signing certificate against the installed app, and only then opens the system installer through a FileProvider limited to the cache folder `updates/`. Offline or on any server fault the check is silent and the app is never blocked; after a failed required update the screen offers "Use the app for now". **What's new (BTL-20261023-1438):** the notes ship inside the app as `app/assets/changelog.json` (one entry per versionCode, `en` and `fil` lines), so they match the running build and need no network. `core/changelog.dart` holds the pure rules (parse, `selectUnseen`, `selectFor`, `decideFirstRun`); the preference `changelog.seen` is the last build whose notes were seen. `main.dart` decides it once before any update check: no value and no `update.lastCheck` is a fresh install (current build counts as seen), no value with `update.lastCheck` is an upgrade from before the feature (seen = 0). The chat screen shows the sheet once and stores the value however it is closed. `release-app.ps1` validates the entry (one per versionCode, non-empty en and fil, notes under 2000 characters) before any ssh or build and checks the asset is inside the APK; empty `-Notes` default to the English lines.

## Agent

DeepSeek or Anthropic tool calling behind a proxy (keys only in server env, per-user and global spend caps, separate chat and memory budgets, one provider per person chosen in settings). The caller identity is injected by the loop. Web text, other people's calendar text and OCR text are untrusted: after they enter a turn, writes are disabled or forced through approval. The fetch tool blocks private addresses. The model payload carries amounts and labels, never account numbers.

- **The turn** (BTL-20261026-2316): at most 20 model calls, 40 tool calls and 16000 characters per tool result in one message, with a note "N tool calls left" from 75% on. `TURN_TOKEN_BUDGET` (charged tokens, default 300000) and a size guard on the prompt close the tools and tell the last call to answer from what it has and say what it could not check. A final reply with no words in it (stray dates, markup) is replaced by a plain note of what ran, and a used-up monthly allowance says so in words. The model's thinking runs at `CHAT_EFFORT` (default high) with up to `CHAT_MAX_TOKENS` (default 64000) per call, on both providers, with the tools offered while it thinks.
- **Long chats**: every finished message stores its tool rounds in `chat_turn.transcript` (results shortened to 3000 characters with a marker that says they are old, web and calendar results replaced by a stub that names the tool, DeepSeek thinking kept only on messages with tool calls and capped, the provider that wrote each message recorded). History (`agent/transcript.ts`, `agent/chat.ts`) is every finished message after the chat's summary, with its rounds replayed only to the provider that wrote them (otherwise the two texts), held under `HISTORY_TOKENS` (default 40000): over it the oldest messages drop in steps of five so the prefix cache holds, and the two newest fall back to text. The earlier part of a long chat is `chat.summary` (with `summary_through_turn_id`), written in the background by the job `chat.compact` (`jobs/chat.ts`, `agent/compact.ts`): incremental from the previous summary, everything but the last two messages, memory bucket, masked with the private names, never inside a message. A fork copies the rounds and not the summary. The summary and the notebook are system messages after the fixed prefix, so the cache order is tools, prefix, notebook, summary, history, then the per-message context.
- **Calculator and forecast**: sums beyond one small addition go through `calc` (`finance/calc.ts`, decimal.js with 40 digits and half-up rounding, a hand-written parser with no eval, limits on length, lines and exponent, a spreadsheet's `pmt`, `fv`, `pv` and `nper`, Manila date helpers). `cashflow_forecast` (`finance/forecast.ts`, `ops/forecast.ts`) works out the balances of every wallet from today to a date (at most 366 days) by day, week or month from the ledger, the unpaid bills with their per-date changes, the paydays and the approved rules as moves; wallets are never added across currencies, a wallet with no rate is named and left out, and moves never change the total.
- **Goals**: `goal_delete` is free for the assistant (after untrusted text or past three deletes in a message it is a card) and removes the goal with every payday rule that points at it in one transaction; no money moves. A payday split skips a rule that points at a switched-off goal and says so.
- **Tool list**: the registry's verbs except the person-only ones and the app-only ones (`agent: false`: the notebook reads, saves and restores, and the old memory verbs, which stay over HTTP for older apps).

## Memory: Botler's Notebook

One Markdown document per person (`notebook`, text up to 16000 characters, a version), always in the prompt as the second system message and the same for every call of one message. The person edits it in the app (the whole text, saved from the version they opened, a stale one refused) and the assistant edits it with `notebook_edit` (add a line under a heading, replace or remove exact text that appears once; a free verb that becomes an approval card after untrusted text, and the card shows the words). Every change keeps the replaced text in `notebook_revision` (newest 50, with who changed it) and can be restored. The first read builds a draft from the old persona, about lines and live facts, and the one-time job `notebook.seed` tidies it as a new version so the raw draft stays restorable. The fact pipeline is retired: no extractor, no digests, no night job, no embedder at start, and queued jobs of those kinds close without running; old facts and digests stay in the database, unused, and export, wipe and data reset still reach them. The private names stay a list (`memory_profile.people`) because the masker needs it. A memory wipe erases the notebook and its changes and clears the old persona and about lines; the names stay. Both tables are exempt from the audit trail (personal text with its own history) and listed with a reason.
